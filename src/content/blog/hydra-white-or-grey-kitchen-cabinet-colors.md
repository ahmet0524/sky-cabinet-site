Cabinets cover more of the kitchen than any other surface. Change the cabinet color and the whole room changes with it: the light, the mood, even how big the space feels. So it is no surprise that color is the decision people spend the most time on in our Norfolk showroom.

Our three best-selling colors are **Hydra**, **White** and **Grey**. All three are in stock, which also makes them the fastest to install. Here is what each one does in a real kitchen and how to pick the right one for yours.

## Hydra: a soft water green

Hydra is our number one best seller. It is a soft, light green with a hint of blue, the color of shallow water. It brings a calm, coastal feel to a kitchen without turning it into a theme.

- **Where it shines:** coastal and cottage homes, kitchens with natural wood floors, and rooms with a view of greenery outside.
- **Best door style:** shaker. The shadow line around the center panel gives the green depth. (See our [shaker vs. slab guide](/blog/shaker-vs-slab-cabinet-doors/).)
- **Pairs well with:** white or warm-toned countertops, butcher block, and brass or gold hardware.
- **Keep in mind:** green reacts to light more than white or grey. It reads cooler in north light and warmer in afternoon sun, so test it in your own kitchen before you commit.

Hydra is a good choice if you want color, but still something that feels soft and easy to live with.

## White: bright and timeless

White is our second best seller and the safest choice in any style of home. It reflects the most light, so it makes small kitchens feel bigger and dark kitchens feel brighter.

- **Where it shines:** small or narrow kitchens, rooms with little natural light, and homes where the kitchen opens onto the living area.
- **Best door style:** both. White shaker looks classic, white slab looks crisp and modern.
- **Pairs well with:** almost every countertop. Marble-look quartz keeps it light. Dark granite or butcher block adds contrast.
- **Keep in mind:** white shows splashes and fingerprints near the stove and the trash pull-out. A quick wipe fixes it, but expect to wipe more often than with grey.

White is the right choice if you want the kitchen to stay fresh for many years, or if you plan to add color through the backsplash, walls or accessories instead.

## Grey: a modern neutral

Grey is our third best seller. It sits between white and a bold color: calmer than white, more neutral than green. It gives a kitchen a modern, finished look.

- **Where it shines:** open plan homes, newer builds, and kitchens with plenty of daylight.
- **Best door style:** shaker for a transitional look, slab for a clean modern one.
- **Pairs well with:** white quartz and brushed or black hardware. A white countertop against grey cabinets is a classic, clean combination.
- **Keep in mind:** grey can feel heavy in a small, dark kitchen. If light is limited, use grey on the lower cabinets or the island and white on the uppers.

Grey hides everyday marks better than white, which makes it a practical choice for busy family kitchens.

## Four questions that make the choice easy

**1. How much light does the kitchen get?** Little light points to White. Good daylight opens the door to Grey or Hydra.

**2. What stays in the room?** Floors, the countertop you keep, the color of the living area next door. Hold the cabinet color against these, not against a white wall.

**3. How long do you plan to live with it?** If you want to change the look later without new cabinets, White or Grey is easier to restyle with paint, tile and hardware. If you want the kitchen itself to be the color, choose Hydra.

**4. Would two colors work better than one?** Two-tone kitchens are very common now. Popular mixes are White uppers with Grey or Hydra lowers, or a Hydra island in an otherwise White kitchen. Ask our designer to show you a two-tone version in your 3D design.
