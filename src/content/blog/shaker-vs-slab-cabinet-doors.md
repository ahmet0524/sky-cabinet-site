The door style sets the look of the whole kitchen. Cabinet boxes hide behind it, the countertop sits on top of it, and the hardware is screwed into it. That is why most kitchen projects in our Norfolk showroom start with the same question: **shaker or slab?**

Both are excellent choices. They just suit different homes, different habits and different tastes. Here is how they compare and how to decide.

## What is a shaker door?

A shaker door is a **five-piece door**. Four flat pieces form a frame (two vertical stiles and two horizontal rails), and a fifth flat panel sits recessed in the middle. That simple square frame is the whole design. There are no curves, no carving and no raised profile.

Shaker is the most requested door style in our showroom, and it is easy to see why:

- **It fits almost any home.** Shaker looks at home in a classic colonial, a coastal cottage and a modern renovation. Hardware and color decide which way it leans.
- **It forgives everyday wear.** The frame breaks up the surface, so small dings and fingerprints are less noticeable than on one large flat panel.
- **It holds color well.** The shadow line around the center panel gives painted colors depth. Soft greens, whites and greys look especially good on a shaker frame.

The trade-off: the inside edge of the frame is a small ledge, and dust and kitchen grease can collect there. A quick wipe with a damp cloth along the edge takes care of it, but it is one more corner to clean.

## What is a slab door?

A slab door is a **single flat panel** with no frame and no recessed center. It is the cleanest, most minimal door you can put on a cabinet.

- **It reads as modern.** Slab doors give a kitchen a calm, uninterrupted look. They are the natural choice for contemporary and minimalist designs.
- **It is the easiest to wipe down.** With no edges or grooves, one pass of a cloth cleans the whole door.
- **It lets the material speak.** Wood grain, a textured finish or a bold color covers the full face of the door with nothing breaking it up.

The trade-off: a large flat surface shows everything. Fingerprints, smudges and small dings are more visible, especially on dark or glossy finishes. Many people pair slab doors with long bar pulls so hands touch the hardware, not the door.

## Shaker vs. slab at a glance

| | Shaker | Slab |
|---|---|---|
| Look | Classic, transitional, coastal | Modern, minimal |
| Construction | Five-piece frame with recessed center panel | One flat panel |
| Cleaning | Wipe the inside edge of the frame | One flat wipe |
| Hides wear | Better | Shows smudges and dings more |
| Best hardware | Knobs, cup pulls or bar pulls | Long bar pulls or edge pulls |
| Works in small kitchens | Yes, especially in light colors | Yes, very clean and open |

## How to choose for your home

**Look at the rest of the house.** If your home has traditional trim, paneled interior doors or a coastal feel, shaker will tie the kitchen to the rest of the house. If the house is newer, open plan or already minimal, slab will feel more natural.

**Think about how you use the kitchen.** A busy family kitchen with kids and pets is usually happier with shaker, because it hides daily wear. A kitchen that is cooked in less often, or kept very tidy, can enjoy the clean look of slab.

**Decide on hardware early.** Hardware changes the style more than people expect. Shaker with brass knobs looks classic. The same shaker door with black bar pulls looks modern. Slab doors almost always need pulls you can grip easily, so plan them with the door.

**Consider mixing the two.** Some kitchens use shaker doors on the wall cabinets and slab fronts on the drawers. The kitchen keeps the warmth of shaker, and the drawers get a cleaner, easy-to-wipe face. Ask our designer to show you both options in your 3D design.
